# Moku landing page

A standalone, responsive landing page for Moku. Built with semantic HTML, CSS, and JavaScript modules, Motion, and Three.js; Vite handles local development and production builds.

```sh
cd website
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Preview: http://127.0.0.1:5186

```sh
npm run build
npm run preview
```

The production build is in `dist/`. It can be hosted as a static site (for example, with Vercel using `website` as the root directory, `npm run build` as the build command, and `dist` as the output directory).

## Design and behavior

The page, in order (docs/specs/next-wave.md §3, with the friends first-class from docs/specs/characters-perfect.md):

1. **The hero:** "Drift less, without a timer.", the line under it, the button and one line on privacy, beside a quiet Mac with the companion small in its corner, filmed by one camera, and a face per companion beside it. Described below.
2. **Moku and Tetsu** (02): "Who'd you like to work with?", the app's welcome question, with the bunny and the robot (Charles dropped the cat on 2026-10-04). Described below.
3. **A daily home** (03): the 24-piece cottage, a live Three.js diorama exported from the app's `world/dayhouse` kit, built by the page's scroll on desktops; another companion's home as the app's renders. Described below.
4. **Your day** (04): the app's Your day window (`Today.swift`, by task with the workflow), drawn by `src/your-day.js` from an example day in `src/your-day-data.js` for each kind of work in the hero (Writing, Design, Code, Study): the date and the companion's note naming the piece it just put up, today's home as it stands with its progress, the day's numbers and ribbon, then each task with its hours, a line about it, the workflow (the apps in the order the work reached them, with minutes, and the detours where they happened), the steps in plain words, the longest stretch and how you came back; the small tasks in one line, and the best comeback. It follows the hero's companion and its kind of work, and its own "A day of" tabs pick the work in the hero too. Generic apps and sites only; the numbers add up (`your-day.test.js`).
5. **Questions:** four answers. "Can Moku see what's on my screen?" is the full privacy answer; the hero's "What Moku reads" link opens it.
6. **The closing:** "Today's house is waiting." (or rocket, or castle) over today's fresh plot, with the companion beside it and one button.

One choice of companion runs the whole page (`src/main.js`): picked in the hero or among the friends, it wears its palette, takes the hero's corner, builds its home in 03, writes Your day's note and waits in the closing. Moku is the default and greets every visit; the choice isn't remembered.

- Warm paper, forest green, sage, DM Sans and Instrument Serif. Fonts, images and clips are served locally. The only runtime requests are the visit counts to the relay (`src/visits.js`) and Vercel's page views and speed on hellomoku.com itself (`src/vercel-insights.js`); no forms.
- Moku's clips are captured from the app's native RealityKit renderer (see Native motion export) as transparent one-shot WebP. A clip stays visible until its replacement is decoded, fades in over the poster and back out into it (120 ms), and holds its last frame for 40 ms before the poster takes over.
- "Pause little movements" (`src/motion-settings.js`) applies to the demo and the page choreography and is remembered locally. The system's reduced-motion preference turns the demo into three stills (the work, the camera's close-up of the sad face, the piece landed, with their captions; no camera moves) and releases the pinned scroll story.
- A small chapter rail follows the reader (01 Hello, 02 Friends, 03 Home, 04 Your day); below 960px it is a dock at the bottom of the screen, hidden while the hero shows.
- The `[data-download]` buttons link to `/download`, which `vercel.json` redirects to the relay's current DMG. Phones and tablets (no hover, coarse pointer) get "Send me the link for my Mac", a `mailto:` to themselves; nothing is collected.

Product copy follows the app's current behaviour and describes the beta honestly. Pricing is omitted because the launch plan still lists it as a decision. The fonts, in full, and their licenses are in `fonts/`; the headline's serif is subset to the characters the page holds (`scripts/subset-fonts.py`, which `npm test` checks new copy against).

## The hero: a live demo

An 18-second loop plays from the first paint on a quiet Mac, in one of four kinds of work (`scenes` in `src/demo-timeline.js`, the apps in `src/demo-scenes.css`), each with its own drift waiting behind it in a browser: **Writing** (an essay typed live; a feed), **Design** (a canvas: the button selected, nudged up three times and filled, later the title retyped; a video, then the next one), **Code** (an editor finishing a function, then the terminal's tests that failed passing; a keyboard in a shop, tried in two colours) and **Study** (a paper highlighted, with margin notes; the news). Generic apps and sites, no brands. A visit plays one kind of work, drawn once per tab session (`sessionStorage`; a reload keeps it); "Writing · Design · Code · Study" above the screen marks it and picks another. No timer, progress bar or caption runs (Charles, 2026-10-05: "three timers and progress are running and distractions kinda in the demo"). One camera films it (2026-10-05, "Camera + it notices you"): the desk is a wide shot, the companion small in its corner. A click brings the drift forward and the wallpaper greys; the companion sniffs the new window; then the camera pushes in on its corner (one scale on the whole desk, 1 to 2.2× over 1.2 s, ease in-out) as its face falls, the windows sliding out of frame, and holds the sad face with nothing else in frame for about two seconds. A click on the work brings it back: the camera pulls back (0.85 s, a soft-start ease-out) as colour returns and the face lifts; the companion cheers and the piece lands beside it, in the same glance. The menu bar clock and the home chip change only then, and the work picks up after it. One quiet line under the stage: "Look away. It'll notice." The companions are a face and a name each, stacked on the page beside the screen's left edge, in the work tabs' style. On desktops the screen is a 16:9 Mac screen (Charles: "a proper 9:16 ratio demo screen"), as wide as the column the headline leaves, but never taller than .86 × the smallest of 58% of the hero's width, the window's height less 306 px over .86, and 920 px, so the whole hero stays above the fold; where the height is the limit, the demo is centred in its column. The scene is sized from the screen's height (`--stage` in `live-demo.css`, from `cqh`) and laid out for 16:9: the windows a little taller and a quarter wider, right of centre in the desk; the house card top right; the companion in its corner, a quarter smaller than before.

- `src/demo-timeline.js` is the script, as data and pure functions: the beats, the camera (`cameraAt`: 0 the wide shot, 1 the close-up), the quiet line, the companion's cues, each scene's tracks (front window, typing, scrolls, `state:`, `var:` and `text:` tracks that the scene's CSS reads, the pointer's moves) and a sped-up day. Work runs as a time-lapse; the drift runs at 8×, so Moku's reactions keep the app's real order (`BehaviorLadder.swift`): sad after the grace second, and the drift ends before the one-minute mark where Moku would turn cross. What the menu bar and the home chip show (`shownDay`) changes only at the payoff: 2h 39m to 2h 40m, the 16th piece, the comeback counted, the clock a minute on. The menu bar says "sped up".
- `src/live-demo.js` applies the state each frame, writing only transforms, opacity and a few text nodes; windows change by data attribute. The camera is one `scale()` on `.desk-inner` about a fixed point, measured so that in the close-up the face sits a little right of centre with the windows out of frame (on a 1 : .86 tablet screen the face moves left to keep them out); the wallpaper reaches a quarter past the screen's right and bottom edges for it. Its zoom is what the companion's 640 px clips can fill on the screen (1.6 to 2.2×). Typed characters only change colour and the caret moves by transform, so typing causes no reflow or layout shift. Clips load after the first paint in the order the loop needs them; the loop starts once the first beat's clips are ready, and enters each later beat only when its clips have arrived. It pauses off screen, in a hidden tab and with the pause toggle.
- **The tab-leave moment ("it notices you"):** leave the tab, take the pointer off the page for over 0.6 s (where there's a pointer), or scroll the demo fully out of view, and the companion notices: the loop holds, the screen greys and the camera holds the close-up of its sad face ("Moku noticed you left."). Out of sight (a hidden tab, scrolled away) it cuts straight to the close-up, so the first frame back is already it; in sight (the pointer) it plays out: the face falls through its own clip (from a cheer too) as soon as the companion is free, and the camera closes in. While it waits it blinks now and then, and the stage draws no frames. Come back (the demo half in view again, for a scroll) and the loop's comeback is yours: a beat of the sad face (at once, if you watched it all along), the camera pulls back, the line says how long you were away ("Gone 14 seconds. You came back."), the companion says "There you are!" as it cheers, and the piece lands. Only leaving the tab changes its title ("Moku noticed…") and favicon (`public/favicon-sad.svg`), so a bookmark or a share made with the pointer up there keeps the real ones. With reduced motion or a pause, only the title and icon change.
- `src/demo-actor.js` plays the cues through the native clips and never cuts one mid-motion: mood changes are the seams, a late mood change still plays, a late gesture is skipped. Its live reactions (`react`) turn the companion to a mood outside the loop through its own transition clip, for the tab-leave moment.
- `src/companions.js` makes a companion data: its clips for each moment, its moods, theme variables and home, and who it is for the friends (species, personality, its built talents, and `arriving` while today's download doesn't have it) (`docs/specs/moku-and-friends.md`). `vite.config.js` looks in `public/` at build time: a companion joins the hero's switcher once its clips are in `public/motion/<id>/` (`scripts/export-motion.py --companion <id>`), and its home section uses its own renders once they're in `public/images/house/<id>/`. With Moku alone the switcher isn't shown. `?companion=<id>` starts with another companion.
- On phones the stage is the same Mac, scaled down, with the same camera; what a visitor reads on it is set in px, 11 or more, and the companions are a row under the quiet line. The H1, the button and the privacy line come first. The small reduced-motion stills beside each other (600 px and up) are close-ups of the companion; a phone's stills stack, each the whole Mac, the middle one in the camera's close-up.
- Screen readers get a description of the demo; the welcome back ("Gone 14 seconds. You came back.") is announced politely.
- `?still=<seconds>` renders a single frame of the writing, which `scripts/export-og-image.cjs` uses for `public/og-image.jpg` (the comeback, at 13.4 s).

- **Switching companions:** the screen and the corner fade out for 0.2 s and the loop starts over just before the hello (`PICKED_AT`, the hello loop in `cueRuns`), so your pick greets you, then acts out the drift and the comeback within seconds.
- **Tetsu's talents,** as the app plays them (`RobotTalents.swift`): once a loop, while you work, its scan clip (`content-scan`, its display narrowing and sweeping) plays and the scan beam sweeps the work window from its visor, 2.2 s from high on the window to its foot (in place of the glance, so right after the work changes; after its hello in the loop that opens with one). The beam's length, sweep and timing live in one place, `scanBeam` in `companions.js`, to match the app's; when the piece lands it projects today's rocket over its head (the app's `world/tetsu/hologram/stage-<n>.png`, in `public/images/house/tetsu/hologram/`), with the app's flicker, bob and 4.6 s. On phones the beam is left out and the hologram floats by the sun.

## Moku and Tetsu

`src/friends.js` and the `#friends` section in `index.html`: the two side by side, each card in its own palette (set from `companions.js`) with the companion in front of its finished home, playing its own clips. As they come into view they say hello in turn, 0.45 s apart, as the welcome's tiles do (`Onboarding.swift`), with "Hi! I'm …"; then each lives a little while you look (a blink, a slow blink, an ear flick, a sniff, a paw), one move at a time, at its own pace. Each card has a personality line, what it builds and, for Tetsu, its built talents (Moku's aren't built yet, so they aren't claimed). The whole card picks; the picked one says hello again. "Watch … notice a drift" goes back up to the hero. On phones the cards are a row you swipe, and each says hello when it's well in view.

The friends' clips load after the hero's loop has (`liveDemo.ready`), or as soon as the section shows. Paused or reduced motion, the cards show their stills. A companion without exported clips keeps its still and can't be picked. Tetsu says "Next update" (`arriving`) because 0.1.7, today's download, has Moku alone: delete it from its card and `companions.js` once the release with Tetsu is out.

## Slow networks

What a visit loads depends on the screen and the connection (`src/loading-plan.js`, docs/specs/next-0-1-9.md §3): `navigator.connection` where the browser has it, elsewhere (Safari, Firefox) the rate of the page's first downloads, lowered by any slow clip download after. The plan only ever gets lighter during a visit.

- **Clips:** a desktop or tablet on a good connection plays the 640 px originals, all fetched at once, exactly as before. A phone (under 600 px wide, or under 500 px high) plays the 320 px copies (what its corner shows in the wide shot, at 3×) and, like any visit with Save-Data or a slow connection (under 1.5 Mbps, or 2G or 3G), fetches only three clips ahead of the ones playing; the tab-leave moment's clips wait until the loop comes round. `src/native-motion.js` picks the file and keeps the window; the friends' cards keep the full-size clips unless the connection is slow.
- **Stills first:** the hero's first still is asked for before the CSS (a preload, the 320 px one on phones); the inline CSS paints the text with the page's first 50 KB, the headline's serif inside it (`vite.config.js`, so it never swaps in late). The actor shows a mood's still for any clip that isn't there yet.
- **The house:** with Save-Data or a slow connection the cottage stays its native render with a "Turn it in 3D" button, and its other 24 renders load only as they're shown; elsewhere the 3D loads near its section, as before. The pictures below the fold (the friends' cards, the house, Your day, the questions) load within half a screen (`src/near-images.js`, `data-near-src`), not when Chrome's lazy loading would (up to 2500 px ahead).
- **Caching:** hashed files in `/assets/` and the clips (their manifest gives each URL with `?v=<hash>`) are cached for a year, immutable (`vercel.json`); the HTML, the manifests and the stills are revalidated.
- **The hero's hook:** `plan.light` from `loading-plan.js` (and `onPlanChange`) says to tell the story in stills first: start the loop at once instead of waiting up to 2.5 s for the first clips, and don't hold a beat for a clip on its way. `companion.home.thumb(minutes)` is the 160 px render for the home chip and the piece that lands (4 KB, not 50).

## Scroll experience

`src/page-motion.js` coordinates Motion reveals and native scroll progress; `src/scroll-experience.css` provides the pinned composition and `src/scroll-story.js` the progress calculation.

When motion is enabled and the viewport is at least 960px wide and 620px high, **A daily home** pins over a 200svh track. Scroll progress assembles the 24-piece cottage, changes the viewing angle, then brings in evening and the lights after construction finishes. The slider, replay, drag, keyboard and lighting controls stay usable; deliberate page scrolling resumes the story after a manual adjustment. The diorama renders on demand (it stops when settled, offscreen or hidden), and its code and Draco-compressed models load near the section; the 25 native house renders are its fallback. Below either threshold, or with motion paused or reduced, the section is in normal flow and the cottage plays its day once when visible.

`src/mobile.css` and `src/mobile-world.css` hold the phone and tablet compositions below 960px; `src/sections.css` the Your day card, the companions row and the closing.

## Native motion export

`python3 website/scripts/export-motion.py` (from the repository root) regenerates Moku's clips, lit by the app's own `studio.json`, on macOS 15+ with Xcode command-line tools and Pillow; add `--companion tetsu` for Tetsu's, from `assets/character/<id>/` into `public/motion/<id>/` (Tetsu's add `content-scan`). The page reads only the clip files and each `manifest.json`, so a re-export drops in; `npm test` checks every clip a companion's moments name is there. It copies the current native visual sources into the ignored `website/review/motion-export/` directory, adds a deterministic sampling bridge, and renders the production mesh, materials, facial controls, ear rig, fidgets, and animation clips. Only capture framing changes, in an isolated asset copy. It does not modify the app or its assets. Use `--encode-only` to recompress captured frames. Each frame is due at the floor of its ideal time, so 30 fps clips land on the 60 Hz vsync grid instead of a frame late. Every clip also gets a 320 px copy, `<name>.320.webp`, for phones and slow connections: the same frames resized with premultiplied alpha and encoded like the drift clip (quality 72, sparse keyframes), with the slowest method and alpha at quality 60 (about a third of the original's bytes; the edges look the same under the camera's close-up). `--variants` writes only those copies, from the captured frames or, for a clip with none captured here, the published clip's own frames, leaving the originals byte for byte. The manifest lists each file with `?v=<hash>`. `scripts/house-thumbs.py` makes the house renders' 160 px copies in `thumb/`.

`python3 website/scripts/export-house.py` regenerates all 25 daily-house renders and the build rules from the native app. It uses an isolated package in `website/review/house-export/`, without changing app sources or assets. Use `--encode-only` to recompress existing captures.

The web models can be regenerated with Blender 5.2, from `website/`:

```sh
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/export-house-web.py
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/export-companion-web.py
```

Both scripts read the original assets and write only to `public/models/`. Three.js and Draco license files are in `public/vendor/draco/`.

The inactive website-only raised-paw greeting is authored in `scripts/export-greeting.py` and retained for later color correction. Run it with Blender to inspect neutral and raised-paw previews; add `-- --frames` to render its 90-frame, three-second sequence inside `review/greeting-render/`. Then run `python3 scripts/export-greeting.py --encode-only` to encode its one-shot clip at `public/motion/content-greeting.webp`. It reads only the already exported website companion mesh. The demo's hello uses the native `content-wave` clip.

## Verification

Run these commands from `website/`:

```sh
npm test
npm run build
npm run preview
```

The production preview runs at http://127.0.0.1:4173. Unit tests cover the live demo's timeline (the camera's shape and its sync with the face, the hold and the payoff, a camera that never jumps, the quiet line, a menu bar and home chip that change only at the payoff, the sped-up day staying true to the drift ladder and the daily house, clips that never overlap and moods that chain through the loop seam, windows that change only on a click, and what fades at the seam), the companion's cue choices over three loops, pending clip swaps and cancellation, daily-house construction rules, the companions' data, and scroll-distance calculations.

`node scripts/check-live-demo.cjs http://127.0.0.1:4173/` records three loops of the demo in headless Chromium twice: once for requestAnimationFrame intervals and Chromium's own frame reporter (dropped frames), once as a screencast measuring Moku's and the desk's motion per frame, to find held frames (checked against each clip's decoded frames) and snaps (at clip seams, the loop seam and the tab-leave moment, which it simulates once). It writes `review/live-demo/report.json` and `frames.csv`. `node scripts/export-og-image.cjs http://127.0.0.1:4173/` renders the og:image. Set `PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE` to an existing Playwright installation's absolute path if it is not installed locally.

`npm run check:network` builds the site, serves it on 127.0.0.1:5194 with `vercel.json`'s headers, and loads it as a first visit in headless Chromium on a phone and a desktop, on a fast connection, 4G, slow 3G (400 kbps, 400 ms) and 4G with Save-Data (`scripts/check-network.cjs`). It prints when the first paint, the text and the download button, the hero, the loop and the first clip show, and the bytes before the hero showed and by the end of the first loop, and fails on a missed budget (docs/specs/next-0-1-9.md §3): under 400 KB before the hero shows; under 1.5 MB for the first loop on a phone; the text, the button and the hero's stills within 3 s on slow 3G. Run it before a push that touches what the page loads. `--only phone:slow-3g,desktop:fast` picks runs; a URL checks a running server instead. The report, with every request, lands in `review/network/report.json`.

`npm run check:browsers` builds the site, serves it on 127.0.0.1:5192 with `vercel.json`'s headers (`scripts/preview.cjs`, without `upgrade-insecure-requests`, which WebKit applies even to plain-http 127.0.0.1) and opens `/`, `/privacy` and `/changelog` fresh in Chromium, WebKit (Safari's engine) and Firefox, side by side, on seven screens: a 1440×900 Retina desktop, a 1280×720 laptop, an iPad upright (820×1180) and sideways (1024×768), an iPhone (390×844), an iPhone SE (375×667) and an Android phone (412×915), the last five touch screens (`scripts/check-browsers.cjs`). It fails on any console or page error, CSP violation, failed request, sideways overflow, text under 11 px on a touch screen (desktops keep their small labels; they're listed as notes), words spilling out of their pill, tap target under 44 px on a touch screen, image or font that doesn't load, control or image without a name, or layout shift after load (landmarks' layout boxes in every engine, and Chromium's own layout-shift entries). On the home page it also checks that the demo plays, a clip animates, the camera reaches its close-up and the comeback and the piece lands; the tab-leave moment (title, icon, the cut to the close-up, "You came back", the camera pulling back); reduced motion switched on mid-loop and on a fresh visit (the three stills, no loop, nothing endless, no pinned story, only the title on a tab switch); the house's 3D or its fallback; that every control does one thing, the same every time (the work tabs and Your day's, the companion pickers and the friends' cards, pause, each question by tap and by Enter, "What Moku reads", the house slider, replay and lighting); that every link resolves; and on desktops that Tab (Option-Tab in WebKit, as Safari's keyboard users press it) reaches every control with a name and a focus ring. Each device plays one kind of work (`?work=`), so a run is the same every time. Frame sheets (the hero at work, in the close-up, at the comeback and back from another tab; each section, the house story at its start, middle and end where it pins; the reduced-motion stills; the other pages) land in `review/browsers/<engine>-<device>/sheet.png`, with `report.json` beside them. `--engines`, `--devices` and `--pages` pick runs, a URL checks a running server or https://hellomoku.com/, and `--dist <dir>` serves another build (an older one, to compare). Chromium draws WebGL on the Mac's GPU (`--use-angle=metal`), as Chrome does; headless SwiftShader took seconds for the cottage's first frame. An engine that can't start is reported as not run, never as passing: on Charles's Mac (macOS 27), Playwright 1.57's Firefox (build 1497) can't start headless (its software compositor can't map a framebuffer), so Firefox needs a Mac or CI where it starts. `CHECK_TRACE=1` prints each step, to find one that hangs; a run that takes over five minutes fails, naming its step.

For browser verification, watch a full loop of the demo, leave the tab and come back, pause and resume it, then scroll through the house story in both directions. Check the house slider and replay, keyboard navigation, the "What Moku reads" link, and reduced motion. Include 960×620, 1280×720 and 1440×900 desktop viewports and a narrow phone viewport; the page should have no horizontal overflow.
